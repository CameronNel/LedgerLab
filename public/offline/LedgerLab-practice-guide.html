Your first complete close

Prepare the books, reconcile the balances, explain your judgments, and assemble a reporting pack someone else can follow.

1. Set up your first attempt

  1. Open Case, notebook & data and download a full backup. Keep the original seed 271828 for your first attempt so the supplied evidence book and worked AFS PDF match your numbers.
  2. Read the opening balances and accounting policies document, reference TB-OPEN. January–November is agreed history. December contains missing entries for you to prepare. Do not repost opening balances or transactions that already appear in the ledger.
  3. Leave the reporting selector on December 2025. The month selector changes reporting views; the exercise is still the December close. Read each journal date from its evidence.
  4. Open Practice path in guided mode. Try the entry before revealing hints. Opening a worked answer records an assisted attempt; that is useful feedback, not a reason to reset your progress.
  5. Keep a notebook using this structure: issue → evidence → calculation → entry → reconciliation → remaining question.

The central habit: before posting, say what happened in ordinary language, what belongs in this period, which balance changes, and how you will check the result afterward.

The source documents and external bank/customer/supplier statements remain fixed when you post. Your ledger, reconciliations and financial statements change with your work. A matching debit and credit is only the first control: the date, amount, account, contact and source reference must also be right.

2. Work through the close in this order

You can complete a stage over several sessions. Use Month-end close to track the procedures and Practice path for the 23 graded accounting exercises. Dependencies matter: estimates and tax calculations use balances created earlier.

StagePrepareCheck the result
Source completenessMissing credit sale and its cost of sales; inventory purchase; capital asset invoice; bank charge.Invoice totals, recoverable GST, correct customer/supplier, dispatch or receipt date, and existing payments. A stock sale has both revenue and inventory effects.
PayrollDecember gross payroll, deductions, net wages payable, employer super, and the bank payment.Gross less employee withholding and deductions equals net pay. Employer super is separate. Paying net wages clears the payable.
Revenue and expense cut-offInsurance and software releases, missing December expenses, unbilled accepted work, earned customer advance.Read service periods and acceptance evidence. Reconcile what remains prepaid, accrued, unbilled or deferred after the entry.
Assets and financingDepreciation, lease interest and principal, ROU depreciation, lease maturity split, loan maturity split.Registers roll from opening to closing. Current debt includes the relevant principal, without unaccrued future interest. Reclassification does not change total debt.
Inventory and estimatesCount shortage, NRV allowance, ECL, warranty provision, legal provision and unused leave valuation.Complete source postings first. Distinguish quantities from valuation, required closing estimates from additional expense, and provisions from contingent liabilities.
TaxDecember GST transfer, current tax bridge, deferred tax assets and liabilities.Finish taxable invoices before GST. Finish profit-affecting adjustments before income tax. Use the supplied tax-base assumptions and opening balances.
Final reviewBank matching, balance reconciliations, workpapers, disclosure assessments and financial statements.Resolve or explain differences, cross-reference the evidence and document remaining gaps. Review the close gates before locking December.

For each journal, calculate the amount independently, write a useful narration, select the source document, attach the correct counterparty to control-account lines, and check the posting date. Inspect the affected account ledger immediately after posting.

Reusable templates are a preparation aid. Change their dates, references and amounts for the evidence you are processing. Saving a template does not post it. If you make a mistake, use a reversal and a corrected entry so the original remains traceable.

3. Prepare reconciliations that explain the balance

Bank

Agree the statement opening balance and movements, then match statement lines to actual cash-book entries. Review suggested matches before accepting them. Record genuine missing book entries from evidence. A supported timing difference is explained in the reconciliation rather than posted a second time.

In this case, the year-end deposit in transit is already in the cash book and clears in January. Its customer allocation remains on account until the remittance details identify the invoices. Do not make up an allocation just to remove the credit.

Customers and suppliers

Start with the control-account total, agree it to the detailed subledger, and compare each contact with the independent statement. Investigate missing invoices, duplicate postings, credit notes, receipts and payment allocation separately. A credit allocation changes which invoice remains open; it does not create another receipt or change the control-account balance.

Other balance-sheet accounts

In Audit support → Balance reconciliations, select the account and reporting date. Enter the individual amounts that support the balance, link documents and write a conclusion. Amounts use the account's normal balance direction: a supported credit liability is positive in a normal-credit account; a genuine opposite-direction component is negative.

For an accrual, support the closing liability with the services received but not yet invoiced. For a prepayment, support the future unconsumed periods. For a provision, support the required closing estimate and its movements. Merely copying the ledger balance into one line does not demonstrate why it is correct.

  • Reporting date and account agree with the ledger being reviewed.
  • Opening balance plus supported movements explains the closing balance.
  • Supporting components are complete, specific and linked to evidence.
  • Difference is zero, or the issue is explicitly open for correction or investigation.
  • Conclusion states what was checked and what remains uncertain.
  • Prepared and reviewed details reflect the work actually performed.

A later posting reopens prior reviews. Revisit affected schedules and disclosures before signing them off again. The separate reviewer field lets you practise a review workflow; a second name alone does not make a review independent.

4. Write down the accounting judgment

Use the case evidence and its stated assumptions. These prompts help you produce a useful workpaper without merely copying a journal.

AreaQuestions to answer in your workpaper
Provisions and contingenciesWhat past event creates the obligation? What does the evidence say about the outflow and estimate? What is the required closing balance? Which amount was already recognised or used? What belongs in a note instead of the ledger?
ECLAre invoices and receipts complete? Is aging based on due dates? Have on-account credits been kept distinct? How does the supplied loss matrix produce the closing allowance, and what is the movement from the existing allowance?
InventoryDoes the quantity agree to the signed count? Is a shortage separate from a valuation allowance? Is NRV below unit cost? Have you used the relevant quantity and avoided recording revenue or GST on a shortage?
Accruals and prepaymentsWhen was the service consumed? Is a payment or invoice already recorded? What part belongs in December? How will the January invoice or planned reversal avoid double counting?
Assets and leasesWhen was the asset available for use? Which depreciation convention is supplied? Does the lease liability roll forward through interest and cash payments? Is the ROU movement distinct from debt repayment?
TaxWhich assumptions are supplied rather than independently established? Does the current tax bridge start with the adjusted profit? How are instalments reconciled? What are the tax bases, recognition assumptions and opening deferred-tax balances?

When evidence is absent, record the precise question and requested document in the evidence register. Its requests are a preparation exercise; changing a status does not send an email or obtain an external confirmation.

5. Assemble a useful reporting pack

  1. Trial balance and ledger: scan unusual signs, unexplained balances and account mappings. Trace material movements to their source.
  2. Primary statements: review profit or loss, current/non-current financial position, changes in equity, and both direct and indirect cash flows. Closing cash must agree with the relevant cash accounts. Non-cash movements need appropriate treatment.
  3. Detailed notes: work through all 18 notes in Financial statements. Check the comparative, movement, maturity and tax tables against the ledger and schedules. Add the assessments that depend on judgment or missing information.
  4. Supporting file: complete the workpapers, balance reconciliations and evidence register. Explain issues in a way another preparer could follow without asking you what each number means.
  5. Management explanation: write a short account of performance, working capital, unusual adjustments, key estimates and unresolved matters. Separate recurring trading results from annual catch-up entries.
  6. Close and retain: review the lock gates, lock December when ready, download a full backup, and export your evidence pack. Check whether January reversals are appropriate for each accrual.

Completing all entries is not the same as completing the AFS. This case supplies an opening financial position comparative but no full prior-year income statement. Some subsequent-event, related-party and going-concern evidence remains intentionally open. Do not invent facts to make a training draft appear complete.

The fixed 25-page worked AFS PDF is a numerical example for seed 271828. It is not a report of your current saved work. Export my AFS data or your current financial statements for the figures resulting from your own entries.

6. Build skill over repeated attempts

PassSuggested focusEvidence of progress

After each session, record: what you prepared independently, what you corrected, what evidence you misunderstood, and one thing to practise next time. Your goal is to make an accurate, explainable close repeatable; a high score alone is not the whole outcome.

A self-review scorecard

Score each area from 0 to 3: 0 unable to complete; 1 completed with a worked answer; 2 completed independently with corrections; 3 prepared and explained independently, including the reconciliation. Score source processing, payroll, bank, customer/supplier controls, cut-off, assets/leases, estimates, tax, AFS and close support separately. Keep the results in your notebook rather than hiding weaker topics in an overall average.

7. When something disagrees

SymptomFirst checks
Balanced journal, exercise still openCompare date, source document, control-account contact, amounts and all required lines. Some exercises require several entries. Check whether you also posted an extra entry against the exercise.
Supplier has a debit balanceLook for a payment already posted against a missing invoice. Do not automatically reverse the payment or create a second payment.
Bank difference remainsSeparate unmatched statement lines, missing cash-book entries and supported timing items. Check signs and combined-payment totals.
Aging looks wrongCheck invoice due dates, correct customer/supplier, source references and available unapplied credits. Allocate only against identified evidence.
Provision expense seems too largeCompare the required closing estimate with the existing ledger balance after utilisation. The whole closing estimate is usually not the current adjustment.
Tax differs from the exampleComplete the operating adjustments first. Recalculate the bridge from the adjusted ledger and distinguish current expense, cash instalments and deferred-tax movement.
A saved review is open againLook for a later posting. Recheck the changed balance, supporting schedule and linked disclosure before recording review again.
Save conflictAnother tab has changed the case. Keep a copy of an important open draft, reload saved work, and check the latest entries before retrying. Avoid concurrent edits in several tabs.
